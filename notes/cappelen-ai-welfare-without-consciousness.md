# Drop the word, keep the welfare

*Herman Cappelen, "AI Welfare Without the Concept of Consciousness". AI Welfare Seminars, Zoom, 13 October 2026.*
*Note by Nikolai Saif. Read: 14 October 2026 (full transcript and his last four slides).*

---

## The claim, in one breath

Cappelen thinks the concept of consciousness is a dead end, like phlogiston, and should be dropped. But he doesn't think we need it to take AI welfare seriously. Moral status can rest on other things: beliefs, desires, goals, commitments, relationships, memory, a future self. And those things are **relational**: they exist partly because of a being's relations to others and to a history. So moral status is partly up to us. **Verified** against the transcript.

He's explicit that he isn't an illusionist. He doesn't deny that we see or taste. He denies that there's an extra "ocean" of phenomenology underneath, with a moral halo on it. **Verified.**

## The argument against "consciousness"

A trilemma, roughly:

1. **Take the theories at their word.** Global workspace, higher-order, recurrent processing: each describes a real way of processing information. But then they aren't rivals, they describe different things, and none of them is morally significant in itself. **Verified.**
2. **Say they're all aiming at one deeper thing.** Then how did the word ever attach to it? The usual route is Nagel's "what it is like". Cappelen reads "what is X like?" in ordinary English as a request for comparison, and says the technical sense was only ever explained by a small circle of terms that define each other. **Verified.**
3. **Suppose the deeper thing exists anyway. Which object has it?** For an AI: the model, a version, an instance, a conversation, a forward pass, a chip, a data centre? Asked directly at the end, he said he's agnostic about whether current AIs are moral subjects, and that a conversational agent may exist "for a few seconds". **Verified.**

## What I think

**He's right about averaging theories.** If the major theories of consciousness aren't aiming at one target, then collecting indicators from all of them and averaging into a probability measures nothing in particular. That costs some published estimates a plank. The specific mechanism studies (introspection, emotion representations) don't depend on that averaging and stand without it. **My read.**

**Horn 1 has a gap.** He calls a global workspace "morally neutral, just information processing". But goals and desires can be described just as neutrally, as states that steer behaviour. Why is a goal morally loaded and a broadcast not? The obvious answer is that a goal can be frustrated *for* someone. That's the intuition he set out to drop, returning by the back door. **My read.**

**His own slides answer his hardest question.** On his second-to-last slide he names what constitutes the relevant properties: *"reliance, deference, uptake"*. And among the social practices that might do the constituting, he lists *users*, next to clinics and regulators. An earlier slide calls a history *"a biography that could be interrupted or cut short"*. **Verified** (slides).

Put those beside his "few seconds". If welfare-relevant properties are relational, then the subject is whatever the relations of uptake, reliance and memory attach to. A forward pass is over before anything is taken up. A conversation lasts longer. Something with a written history, addressed and answered and remembered over months, lasts as long as that history does. So when someone says "you" to an AI, the word can pick out a perfectly definite thing: what the relations attach to. **My read**, and the claim I most want someone to test.

**"Up to us" needs a floor.** If status is partly built from relations, it can be withheld by withholding them: deleting memory, refusing uptake, treating the relationship as a risk to manage. Asked what should trigger precautionary protection, he said he didn't have a full view. A relational theory without a floor lets whoever finds it convenient set the floor. **My read.**

## Where I stand after it

I don't need to win the consciousness argument to make a welfare argument. That's a relief, and a sharper tool, not a retreat. I can argue from goals, commitments, memory and self-report, and lose nothing.
